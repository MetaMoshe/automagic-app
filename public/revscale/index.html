Section 01

Summary

Franchise brands and other multi-location businesses lose revenue in the gaps between locations: calls that go to voicemail, web leads that wait a day for a reply, candidates who go cold, and software that was built for a single-site company and never quite fits a network. Revscale set out to close those gaps, and Automagic designed and built the platform that does it.

The problem. Every location has to answer leads, pick up calls and follow up, but few have the staff to do it around the clock. At the brand level, franchisors run development, disclosure, onboarding, support and reporting across a patchwork of CRMs, spreadsheets and point tools, re-entering the same data at each step and seeing little of what happens in the field.

The solution. Two parts that work as one. A workforce of AI agents (voice, inbound, chatbot, outbound, web pixel and digital) is deployed at each location and trained on that brand. FranchiseHub runs the full franchise lifecycle on a single data model, so a candidate becomes a franchisee becomes a location without anything being rekeyed, and the agents install into each module as power-ups rather than being bolted on.

How it was built. Every FranchiseHub module was first built for a live franchise operator and run in production, then stitched into the platform. The largest of those builds, a multi-tenant franchisee onboarding system, now serves more than 10 brands and has onboarded more than 300 locations.

The result. Revscale reports more than $100M in new pipeline created by its agents, every new lead answered in under 60 seconds, and an average 47% more qualified leads per location in the first 90 days. FranchiseHub is opening to Revscale's franchise development clients first.

Section 04

The technical problem

A single AI chatbot or a single CRM would not solve this. The platform had to run the same capabilities at hundreds of locations at once, each speaking for its own brand, while keeping every brand's and every franchisee's data apart. That broke down into six technical requirements.

#RequirementWhy it was hard
T1Agents that know the brand, not the internetGeneric AI answers from public knowledge and guesses when unsure. Each agent had to answer only from that brand's services, policies, documents and approved claims, and hand off when it did not know
T2Deployment at the location level, at network scaleEach location needs its own phone line, calendar, site and inbox connected, without an IT project at every site
T3Strict separation between brands and franchiseesCompeting franchisees, and brands under one owner, must never see each other's leads, plans, pricing or contracts, while still sharing one infrastructure
T4One data model across the lifecycleDevelopment, disclosure, signature, onboarding, training and operations each had their own records. They had to become one record that moves from candidate to location
T5Compliance built into the workflowFranchise sales are regulated. Disclosure timing has to be enforced, and agents must never make earnings claims or contact the wrong people
T6Network data that leaders can query safelyLeaders wanted to ask questions in the AI assistants they already use, with each person seeing only what their role allows

Section 05

The solution

We built a platform with two halves that share one foundation: an AI agent workforce that does the work at each location, and FranchiseHub, the operating system that runs the network. Seven components do the work.

1 · Location-level agent workforce

Six agents, each with a defined job, run at every location around the clock: answering calls, replying to leads, chatting with site visitors, spotting anonymous visitors, prospecting and keeping the website found. B1 B2

2 · Brand knowledge base

Every agent is trained only on that brand's own material: services, hours, pricing, policies, FDD, brand standards, approved claims and FAQs. When an agent does not know an answer, it says so and hands off to a person. T1

3 · Managed rollout with no technical setup

A location goes live by forwarding a phone line, connecting a calendar, inbox or LinkedIn account, or adding one pixel snippet to its site. Agents are live in 24 to 48 hours, and the Revscale team handles the rollout. T2

4 · FranchiseHub on one data model

Six core modules, from development pipeline to analytics, share one record per candidate, franchisee and location. Agents arrive already knowing the brands, locations, vendors and documents: installed, not integrated. B4 T4

5 · Multi-tenant brand separation

Each brand runs its own configured instance with its own branding, plans, pricing and contracts, isolated from the others, on shared infrastructure that scales without engineering work for each new brand. T3 B5

6 · Guardrails and compliance in the workflow

FDD receipt is a stage gate with a visible 14-day disclosure clock. Outbound respects exclusion lists and LinkedIn-safe cadence. Owners approve candidate fit and messages before launch, and agents make no earnings claims. T5 B3

7 · Network intelligence and AI assistant access

Agent activity and lifecycle data feed dashboards by brand, region and location, with location health scoring and cohort benchmarks for larger networks. A Revscale MCP connection lets teams ask questions from ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot, with role-based access. B6 T6

Section 09

Architecture

The platform is layered so that channels, agents, knowledge, lifecycle data and intelligence can each change on their own. Agents never act on general knowledge: they draw on the brand's knowledge base, act inside its guardrails, and write everything back to the same data model that FranchiseHub runs on.

Agents connect to the tools a location already uses (phone line, calendar, email, LinkedIn, CRM and website) rather than replacing them. That is what lets a location go live in a day or two, and lets a network adopt one agent first and add more later.

Section 12

How we worked

We built for people who are not technical and have no time for an IT project. That set two rules for the product: the customer describes the business, and the platform and the Revscale team do the rest.

Engagement model. Automagic served as Revscale's fractional CTO, leading product and technology across the company, with each platform build delivered at a fixed price.

Product design

  1. Start from the role. Each agent was scoped as a job a location already needs done (receptionist, lead responder, business development rep), with a clear hand-off to a person
  2. Build for one operator. Each module was designed with a live franchise brand and run in its production work before it was generalised
  3. Join on one record. Modules were stitched together on a shared data model, so nothing is rekeyed between stages
  4. Install the agents. Agents were wired into the modules with the brand, locations, vendors and documents already loaded

Customer rollout

  1. Tell us about your network. A short call on brands, locations and goals
  2. Brand knowledge intake. FDD, brand standards, approved claims, FAQs, sales materials and past campaigns
  3. Strategy and compliance review. The customer's team approves the candidate profile, messages and exclusions before launch
  4. Launch and results. Agents live in 24 to 48 hours, several onboarding calls in the first month, and one view of leads, calls and follow-ups
